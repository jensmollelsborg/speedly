Governance principles

These seven rules make the library safe to scale across a regulated bank. Every skill in the catalogue is built and reviewed against them.

01
One skill, one capability

A skill that does five things is a bad skill. Decompose. Narrow scopes are easier to test, easier to govern, and easier to retire.

02
Cite or refuse

Any skill producing factual claims cites the source or says “I don't know”. No invented citations, no confident paraphrase without a referent.

03
Functions own their skills

Compliance owns the regulatory skills. AML owns KYC. Legal owns contracts. Sign‑off is a hard gate before a skill goes live.

04
Drafts, not decisions

Skills draft. Humans decide. Outputs lead with a DRAFT banner and surface uncertainty rather than papering over it.

05
No client data in unapproved tools

The catalogue runs in Pictet‑approved AI environments only. Skills enforce this in their own instructions and refuse to operate on raw confidential data.

06
Quarterly review

Every skill's owner reviews it quarterly. References (regulator URLs, policy indexes) decay fast — verification is part of the lifecycle, not optional.

07
One contribution path

A single markdown template, a function sign‑off, a central review, a published skill. No shadow library, no rogue forks.

Lifecycle of a skill

The path from idea to published skill — and the periodic review that keeps it honest.

STEP 01

Author

A team member drafts the skill against the catalogue template: name, description, instructions, references, examples.

STEP 02

Function sign‑off

The owning function (Compliance, AML, Legal…) reviews and approves. Sign‑off is recorded — no sign‑off, no publication.

STEP 03

Central review

The AI Centre of Excellence checks alignment with the seven governance principles and tests the skill end‑to‑end.

STEP 04

Publish

The skill ships into the bank's Pictet‑approved Claude environment, with its owner, version, and last‑review date visible.

STEP 05

Quarterly review

Owners verify references, retest behaviour, and confirm the skill is still needed. Retiring a stale skill is a feature.
